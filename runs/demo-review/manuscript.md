## Results and discussion

[P0] **[changed]** Wall wettability affects both the nucleation onset and the later bubble growth [cite]. We now examine its effect on nucleation with molecular dynamics simulations.

[P1] **[changed]** Figure [ref] shows how the vapor fraction near the wall evolves in time. We compare contact angles of 35$^\circ$, 70$^\circ$ and 110$^\circ$. At a wall temperature of 300 K, the onset time of nucleation decreased from 350 ps on the 110$^\circ$ surface to 125 ps on the 35$^\circ$ surface. This indicates that a smaller contact angle accelerates nucleation. The 70$^\circ$ surface gave an intermediate onset time of 210 ps. These results are consistent with the onset time varying monotonically with the contact angle over the range we studied.

[P2] \begin{equation}
 t_{\mathrm{on}} = t_0 \exp\left(\frac{\Delta G^*}{k_B T_w}\right)
 \label{eq:onset}
\end{equation}

[P3] **[changed]** We can interpret the increase in nucleation onset time with contact angle using classical nucleation theory, as expressed in Eq. [ref]. Within this framework, the trend may be attributed to a lower energy barrier $\Delta G^*$ on more hydrophilic surfaces. This lower barrier would come from the enhanced solid--liquid interaction [cite]. This result suggests that interfacial wettability affects the onset of phase change. Under the conditions studied here, wettability could be considered in the design of cooling surfaces for electronic devices.
