# Response to reviewers

## Reviewer 1

**Comment R1.01.** The authors use overstated language such as "clearly demonstrate" and "proves" for results obtained at only three contact angles and one wall temperature. Please temper these claims.

**Response:** We agree and have revised the wording in the Results and discussion. "Clearly demonstrate" is replaced by a plain statement of the onset times at 300 K followed by "indicates", and "proves" is replaced by "consistent with", restricted explicitly to the range of contact angles we studied. We also hedged the classical-nucleation-theory interpretation ("may be attributed") and the design implication, which is now limited to the conditions studied here. The data set itself is unchanged: the results still cover three contact angles and one wall temperature, and the revised text is scoped to that range rather than extended with new simulations.

> This indicates that a smaller contact angle accelerates nucleation. (Results and discussion)

> These results are consistent with the onset time varying monotonically with the contact angle over the range we studied. (Results and discussion)

> Under the conditions studied here, wettability could be considered in the design of cooling surfaces for electronic devices. (Results and discussion)

**Comment R1.02.** The mechanism proposed in the last paragraph (a lower energy barrier on more hydrophilic surfaces) contradicts classical heterogeneous nucleation theory, which predicts a lower barrier on hydrophobic walls. Please justify or revise.

**Response:** The reviewer is right that classical heterogeneous nucleation theory predicts a lower vapor-nucleation barrier on hydrophobic walls, so our proposed mechanism runs against that expectation. In this revision we only softened the wording of the last paragraph: the mechanism is now presented as a possible attribution within the classical framework rather than a demonstrated finding, and the claims about "innovative" results and next-generation cooling-surface design were removed or narrowed. We have not resolved the underlying contradiction: the manuscript still attributes the trend to a lower barrier on hydrophilic surfaces, and it contains no new justification or alternative explanation (for example, kinetic or interfacial-structure effects outside classical theory). Whether to revise the mechanism or add such a justification is a decision the authors still need to make.

> We can interpret the increase in nucleation onset time with contact angle using classical nucleation theory, as expressed in Eq. [ref]. (Results and discussion)

> Within this framework, the trend may be attributed to a lower energy barrier $\Delta G^*$ on more hydrophilic surfaces. (Results and discussion)

> This lower barrier would come from the enhanced solid--liquid interaction [cite]. (Results and discussion)

## Reviewer 2

**Comment R2.01.** The wall temperature and the contact angles should be stated together with the onset times so that readers can compare cases.

**Response:** The wall temperature, the three contact angles and the corresponding onset times are already reported together in the first results paragraph, so readers can compare the cases directly: 350 ps at 110°, 210 ps at 70° and 125 ps at 35°, all at a wall temperature of 300 K. This was also the case in the original submission; the revision rewrote this paragraph for clarity and now opens the onset-time sentence with the wall temperature, but it adds no new information on this point. We did not add a separate table, because only three cases at a single wall temperature are compared. If the reviewer would prefer a table, the authors will need to decide whether to add one.

> At a wall temperature of 300 K, the onset time of nucleation decreased from 350 ps on the 110$^\circ$ surface to 125 ps on the 35$^\circ$ surface. (Results and discussion)

> The 70$^\circ$ surface gave an intermediate onset time of 210 ps. (Results and discussion)
